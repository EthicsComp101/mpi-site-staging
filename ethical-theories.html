Introduction to Ethical Theories

Free9 minutesFor ethics bowl, Olympiad and invitational students

What is an ethical theory, and how can it sharpen your arguments in a competition round? In the first video of our ethics course, Archie Stapleton introduces the three main approaches, consequentialism, deontology and virtue ethics, using one simple question: should you read your girlfriend's diary to find out what she wants for her birthday?

What the video covers

Three lenses for any ethics case

Consequentialism

Right and wrong depend on results. Utilitarianism asks which choice creates the most happiness and least suffering, often through a stakeholder analysis.

Deontology

Some things are required or forbidden whatever the consequences: duties, rules and rights, such as a right to privacy. Kant comes in a later video.

Virtue ethics

From Aristotle. The focus moves from the action to the person: which character traits does a choice show, and where is the golden mean?

Every theory has two parts: a theory of the good (what is valuable in itself) and a theory of right conduct (how to decide what to do).
