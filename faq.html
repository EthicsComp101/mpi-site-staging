FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Answers for teachers and coaches, parents and students about our ethics competitions, the Global AI Debates, coaching and funding. Can't find it here? Contact us.

For teachers and coaches

What happens in an Ethics Olympiad or TKEthics round?›

Students discuss an ethical case and explain their answer to a question about it. One team presents, the other offers comments, and the presenting team responds and answers judges’ questions. The teams then switch roles for another case. See our competition rules for the full sequence and timings.

How does scoring work?›

Judges assess the quality of students’ reasoning, their engagement with other views, their responses and their respectful dialogue. Teams can agree. Thoughtful questions and fair treatment of another argument matter. The aim is a collaborative discussion of the case. Our competition rules explain the scoring categories and points.

How many students can join a team, and what ages can enter?›

Ethics Olympiad and TKEthics teams have three to five students. Age ranges depend on the event. The Middle School Pan Atlantic Ethics Olympiad is for ages 11–14. The TKEthics Spring Invitational is for ages 13–18. Check the event’s eligibility details before choosing your team, especially if students span different school years.

How do online events run?›

Teams join online through Zoom. Before the event, plan a quiet space, a reliable internet connection and a setup where students can hear and be heard clearly. Test the camera and microphone together. Follow the event’s joining instructions and schedule, and make sure everyone knows where to find them on the day.

How should we check the event time in our time zone?›

Start with the time zone stated in the event schedule, then convert it for the event date. Daylight saving changes can alter the difference between countries. Share the local date, start time and expected finish with students and families. Use our event calendar to find the relevant event details.

Who are the judges, and what do they do?›

Our judges are paid philosophers at PhD level. A judge listens to the discussion, asks questions, assesses the team’s reasoning and gives feedback. Students should expect to explain why they hold a view and consider objections. Preparing for that conversation can be as simple as practising clear answers to “Why?” and “What if?”

How can we prepare using the free resources?›

Start with the free case sets and teacher materials at Resources. Read a case together, identify who is affected and compare possible responses. Ask students to give reasons, consider an objection and revise their answer if needed. Then practise a short discussion with another group. Use the event’s assigned cases when preparing for that tournament.

Do I need a philosophy background to coach a team?›

You can begin by helping students read carefully, ask useful questions and explain their reasons. Try one case together before planning a full practice session. Our free teacher resources give you a starting point. If you would like more guidance, explore coaching and classes and choose support that fits your group’s experience.

Which upcoming event should our school consider?›

The online Middle School Pan Atlantic Ethics Olympiad takes place on Saturday 14 November 2026 for ages 11–14. Spring options include the online TKEthics Spring Invitational on Saturday 6 March 2027, the online Senior European Ethics Olympiad on 18 March 2027, and the online Senior Pan American Ethics Olympiad on Saturday 3 April 2027 for high school teams. See the event calendar.

Is there a fee-support form?›

A fee-support form exists at Scholarships. If you are exploring how to fund participation, that is the place to find it. You can also use Fund Your Team when planning a school funding request. Gather your team details and discuss the plan with the adult coordinating participation before completing any forms.

How can we raise funds for a team?›

Use Fund Your Team as a starting point. Consider asking your school’s activities lead, parent association or a local sponsor about support. Explain which event the team wants to attend, who will coordinate it and what students will practise. Agree who will handle requests and payments, and follow your school’s rules for fundraising.

This year’s motion is “AIs should be granted the rights of legal persons.” Live online finals take place on 5 December, with a US$3,500 prize pool. Visit the website for entry instructions and format requirements.

For parents

What will my child practise through these events?›

Students practise explaining an idea, giving reasons, listening closely and responding to questions. Ethical cases also ask them to consider how a decision affects different people. You can support preparation by asking your child to explain a case in everyday language, then describe a reasonable objection to their own view. Our resources offer a place to begin.

Which event fits my child’s age?›

The Middle School Pan Atlantic Ethics Olympiad on Saturday 14 November 2026 is for ages 11–14. The TKEthics Spring Invitational on Saturday 6 March 2027 is for ages 13–18, in teams of three to five. Both are online. Check the chosen event’s eligibility and participation requirements with the teacher or coach coordinating your child’s entry.

My child is interested but has no school team. Where should we start?›

Ask whether a teacher or club leader could help bring a team together. You can also contact MPI to ask about participation options for a student without a school team. Include your child’s age, time zone and event of interest. Please confirm the available arrangements before making plans around a particular event.

What does my child need for an online event?›

Plan access to Zoom, a stable internet connection and a quiet place to take part. Check sound and camera settings before the day. Coordinate the setup with the team’s teacher or coach, including how students will join and communicate. Keep the event schedule handy, and leave time beforehand to resolve any connection problems.

How much preparation should we plan?›

A useful starting point is a regular, manageable practice session. Read one case, discuss possible answers and ask your child to explain their reasoning aloud. Build towards practice with teammates as the event approaches. The right amount depends on experience and school commitments. Use the free resources to plan sessions with the teacher or coach.

How can I help without taking over?›

Ask open questions during preparation: who is affected, what matters here, and what might someone disagree with? Give your child time to work out an answer. Encourage them to explain uncertainty as well as conviction. During an event heat, parents must not communicate with the team. Read the competition rules before the day.

Where can our family find information about fee support?›

A fee-support form exists at Scholarships. If your child is joining through a school, discuss the process with the teacher coordinating the team. For ideas about organising funding, visit Fund Your Team. It helps to agree who will complete forms and manage the team’s arrangements before anyone submits a request.

Does MPI offer coaching or classes?›

Yes. Visit Coaching and Classes to explore MPI’s teaching options. When considering support, think about your child’s experience, interests and schedule, and whether they want help individually or with a team. You can also begin with the free resources and discuss which parts of preparation would benefit from more guidance.

For students

What if I am new to ethics or nervous about speaking?›

Start with one case and one question you care about. Explain your answer to a friend, then ask them what needs more explanation. Practising in a small group can make speaking feel more familiar. You do not need to fill your answer with technical terms. Aim to make your reasons clear enough for someone else to follow.

Do we have to disagree with the other team?›

Teams may agree and can still have a useful discussion. Look for questions worth exploring together: does a reason apply in every case, or would a different fact change the answer? During practice, try identifying a point you accept before raising a question. That helps you respond to what someone actually said.

How should our team divide the preparation?›

Try giving each teammate a different task for the same case: explain the dilemma, identify affected people, suggest an answer or develop an objection. Then compare notes so everyone understands the whole discussion. Rotate tasks during practice. This helps you notice gaps and gives each person experience with more than one part of the argument.

What if a judge asks something I cannot answer?›

Take a moment to think and ask for clarification if you need it. You can explain what makes the question difficult or identify a fact that would affect your answer. In practice, ask teammates questions you have not prepared in advance. Focus on giving a reasoned response and being clear about what you are unsure of.

Where can I find cases to practise with?›

Visit our free Resources page for case sets and preparation materials. Choose a case, write down the main ethical question and list two possible answers. Give a reason for each, then decide which seems stronger and why. Discuss it with a teammate and see whether their questions change your thinking.

Can I enter the Global AI Debates with a speech or an essay?›

Yes. The Global AI Debates accepts a recorded speech or a team essay on AI ethics, due by 21 November 2026. The motion is “AIs should be granted the rights of legal persons.” Live online finals are on 5 December, and the prize pool is US$3,500. Check the website’s format requirements before recording or writing your entry.

What is the Case Prize?›

The Case Prize is a student case-writing competition, with the next round in spring 2027. A case gives readers an ethical situation to think through and discuss. To practise, choose a decision where there are serious reasons for more than one response. Visit the case competition page for information and check the next round’s requirements before submitting.

How do I take the next step?›

Choose an event that fits your age and schedule using the event calendar, then discuss it with a teacher, coach or parent. For a team event, start finding teammates and set a first practice date. If you want guided preparation, explore coaching and classes. You can start reading the free cases while arranging the rest.
