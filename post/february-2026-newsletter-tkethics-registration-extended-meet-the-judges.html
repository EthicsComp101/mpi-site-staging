Meet some judges for the upcoming season

A few of the people who will be asking questions, giving feedback, and helping students sharpen their thinking.

Brian Palmiter

Adjunct Lecturer in Public Policy at Harvard Kennedy School and Civil Discourse Fellow at the Edmond & Lily Safra Center for Ethics. Coaches the Harvard Ethics Bowl Team and advises the Harvard Undergraduate Ethics Society. Researches ethics, democratic theory, and public policy, focusing on how people with divergent values reason together and sustain shared institutions.

Jack M. Wells

PhD student at The New School for Social Research (Aristotle Onassis Prize Fellow). Researches race in international law and early 20th-century global diplomacy. Archivist and longtime Ethics Olympiad judge and facilitator.

"What I love most about judging an Ethics Olympiad is the opportunity to enter into an actual dialogue on ethics with students, as opposed to just "marking" them. It feels like a more humane approach -- I get to put something of myself into my questions and feedback, and they get the opportunity to challenge what I say head-on."

Mika Smith

BA in philosophy and psychology; PhD student in philosophy and science studies. Researches reasoning, values, and methodology in science, including AI. Ethics Olympiad judge focused on student dialogue.

"Philosophy is important in life because it helps us investigate, discover, discredit, or confirm assumptions in institutions, science, and moral practices. It provides tools for careful evaluation and debate, enabling us to improve systems of knowledge and challenge immoral and unjust practices."

"With so much information out there on AI's capabilities and limitations, separating fact from fiction is a critical function of education — which can happen in various ways. Debates like this are one essential avenue to open up the discussion about AI's variegated impacts."
