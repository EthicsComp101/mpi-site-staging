Foundations first, then practice

Every plan is different, but most students follow a path like this. Each stage builds the skills in the one before, and we keep working on thinking, speaking and writing all the way through.

  1. Foundations of thinking

    What an argument is, how to give good reasons, how to spot weak ones, and how to change your mind well. This is where our Critical Thinking & Argumentation class starts.

  2. Big questions

    Students take on the questions philosophers have argued about for centuries, such as what we can know, free will, justice and the good life, and learn to defend their own view.

  3. Speaking and presenting

    Students work through real ethical cases, present a position clearly, consider the strongest objection and answer questions on the spot.

  4. Writing

    Students turn their reasoning into clear writing, from short arguments to full essays, with feedback on structure, clarity and evidence.

  5. Putting it into practice

    When they’re ready, students can test their skills in an online ethics competition or an essay prize, with coaching along the way.

For parents

Does my child need any background in philosophy?

No. We start from where your student is, and our introductory classes assume no prior study.

Can siblings or friends learn together?

Yes. Three or more students who want to learn together can have a course designed for their group.

Can I be involved as a parent?

Yes. We can coach your student directly, or help you run sessions at home with our case sets and materials.

Do you have free materials we can try first?

Yes. Case sets, a competitor handbook and an introductory lecture on the main ethical theories are free on our resources page.

What if cost is a barrier?

Apply through our fee support form. Requests are assessed case by case.
