Sample judging criteria

Ethics Competition Judging Criteria

Each team scores out of 60 per heat. Here's how those points are distributed and what judges look for in each speech.

Score sheetPresentationOther categories
The score sheet

Per-team scoring breakdown (out of 60)

Points are spread across four scored speeches per case, plus a single respectful-dialogue score at the end of the heat. Because each team presents once and comments once per heat, the totals balance: both teams have an equal opportunity to score every available point.

CategoryPointsWhen you earn it
Presentation (the case you present)15When you are the presenting team
Commentary (on the other team's case)10When you are the commenting team
Response to commentary15When you are the presenting team
Response to judges' questions15When you are the presenting team
Respectful dialogue5Once per heat, based on overall conduct
Total60
15 points

How the presentation's 15 points break down

The 15-point presentation score is built from three sub-criteria, each worth 5 points. Judges score them independently and add them together.

Criterion A · 5 pts

Clear and systematic

Was the presentation clearly organized, and did it systematically address the moderator's question? A 5 means an exceptionally clear presentation that addresses the key dimensions of the question. A 1 means an incoherent presentation.

Criterion B · 5 pts

Central moral dimensions

Did the team correctly identify the central moral dimensions of the case and engage them with depth? A 5 means the team named those dimensions precisely and worked through them carefully. A 1 means the team misidentified them.

Criterion C · 5 pts

Awareness of opposing viewpoints

This is the steel-man criterion. Did the presentation show real awareness of the views someone who disagrees with the team would hold? A 5 means full and careful attention to the strongest opposing views. A 1 means minimal awareness of any other viewpoint.

Scored holistically

How the other categories are scored

Commentary, the two response scores, and respectful dialogue are each scored holistically against a single rubric. The headline question for each:

10 pts

Commentary

How directly and effectively did the team engage the presenting team's argument?

15 pts

Response to commentary

How thoughtfully and completely did the team respond to the commenting team's points?

15 pts

Response to judges' questions

How thoughtfully did the team identify and engage the most pressing points raised by the judges?

5 pts

Respectful dialogue

Did the team treat this as a productive, collaborative discussion rather than a combative debate?
