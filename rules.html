Ethics Competition Rules

These rules follow the MPI Competitor Handbook and apply to every MPI ethics event.

The spirit of the event

This is not a debate

Reason together, honestly.

Both teams are working together to think carefully about a hard ethical question, and judges are watching to see which team reasons more clearly and more honestly about it. Teams can agree with each other. Teams that try to score points by attacking the other side tend to do worse than teams that engage seriously with the strongest version of the other side's view.

Teams

Who competes

Three to five students

A team is three to five students.

Roles are flexible

The team divides speaking roles however it wants. No role is fixed to any one person, and the same speaker can take parts across the heat or hand off mid-round.

One speaker or many

Judges neither reward nor penalise a team for having one speaker or many: both approaches are welcome.

How a heat runs

Two rounds, every speech

Two rounds per heat

Every heat is built from two rounds. In Round 1, Team A presents on the first case and Team B comments on it. In Round 2, the roles flip: Team B presents on a second case and Team A comments. By the end of a heat, both teams have delivered every type of speech.

The opening coin toss

Before the heat begins, the moderator runs a coin toss. The winner chooses to be Team A (presents first) or Team B (comments first). You don't see which cases will be in the heat when you're choosing.

Round 1: timing

The moderator distributes the case study and reads the question aloud. Once the question is read, Team A's conference clock starts.

StepWhat happensTime
1Team A confers privately to plan its presentation3 min
2Team A delivers its presentation5 min
3Team B confers privately to plan its commentary1 min
4Team B delivers its commentary on Team A's presentation3 min
5Team A confers privately to plan its response1 min
6Team A responds to Team B's commentary3 min
7Judges question Team A in a back-and-forth conversation7 min

Teams must answer the moderator's question in their presentation; they are scored on how clearly and systematically they address it.

Round 2: roles reversed

Round 2 is identical to Round 1, except now Team B presents and Team A comments. A new case is distributed and the sequence repeats. At the end of the heat, judges add the respectful-dialogue score (given once per heat, not once per round) and total each team's points.

During the heat

Conferences, notes and AI

Conferences

  • During a conference, your team should ideally be in one room together with microphones muted.
  • If team members are in different places, set up a separate channel ahead of time (a side video call or chat) and only use it during your allotted conference times.
  • The other team and any audience members must remain silent during your conference. Written notes between teammates are fine throughout the heat.
  • Keep your microphone off unless your team has been invited to speak.

Materials, notes and AI

  • Notes and prepared materials are permitted.
  • The use of AI is not permitted, and judges will decrease the scores of teams they believe are using AI.
  • We will not enter into correspondence about this, and we do not allow teams to accuse other teams of using AI.

Heats are scored on real-time reasoning under pressure. If it sounds like you're reading something, you'll likely be marked down, regardless of where the text came from. Cases are released ahead of the tournament, but you won't see the moderator's specific question until the heat begins, so prepare a clear understanding of each dilemma and a position you genuinely own rather than a script.

Scoring

Out of 60 per team, per heat

CategoryPoints
Presentation (the case you present)15
Commentary (on the other team's case)10
Response to commentary15
Response to judges' questions15
Respectful dialogue (once per heat, based on overall conduct)5
Total60

How the presentation is scored

The presentation's 15 points are three criteria worth 5 each: A. Clear and systematic (did it address the moderator's question in an organised way?), B. Central moral dimensions (did the team identify and engage them with depth?) and C. Awareness of opposing viewpoints (did it take the strongest opposing views seriously?).

Commentary, the two responses and respectful dialogue are each scored holistically. Commentary asks how directly and effectively the team engaged the presenting team's argument; the responses ask how thoughtfully the team engaged the most pressing points raised; respectful dialogue asks whether the team treated the heat as a productive, collaborative discussion rather than a combative debate.

Officials

Moderators, judges and results

Moderators and judges

  • The moderator keeps the official time. A visible timer is used; if it fails, the moderator gives time warnings.
  • The moderator may let a speaker finish a sentence once time has expired.
  • Judges direct their questions to the team, not to individuals.
  • Coaches, parents and audience members may not communicate with a team during a heat, verbally or non-verbally. The moderator decides whether to pause a round for any disruption.

Results

  • The decisions of the judges are final. Scores are totalled at the end of the day and results are published within 24 hours and emailed to supervising teachers.
  • Ties are broken by the highest score in the first heat, then the second heat, and so on.
  • Any complaint or challenge to the results should be put in writing to the organisers.
